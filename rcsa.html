Monocular Ego-Motion Mapping on a Mobile Robot

← Ivan Torriani

Last updated 10/24/26 — next-capture recommendations posted

A mobile robot that wants to act on its surroundings needs two things from its cameras: where it has been, and what is where. This one — a farm-ng AMIGA driven down rows of cabbage, broccoli and peppers — gets both out of a single moving sensor, with no stereo baseline, no LiDAR and no external positioning, and lands on an object-level 3D map of the row it just drove with one box around each of the 93 plants in it. The navigation problems underneath are the ordinary ones: keyframing, pose estimation, accumulated drift, degenerate motion, and scale. The posts below cover the two tracks in the order they were built — a vision-language baseline on crop and growth stage, then the reconstruction that caught it identifying the wrong crop. Everything runs locally on an Apple M4 with 16GB and no NVIDIA GPU.
