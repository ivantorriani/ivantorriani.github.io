CSAI Research exists to give undergraduates at Cal Poly one of the most professional research experiences they can get. As Director of Research I set the program's direction, pick and scope its projects, and hold each one to the standard of a submitted paper. This year that is three projects, each run by its own research lead, each starting from a problem with a number on it.

The projects

Mind the Noise

Artifact detection and clean-data retention in EEG from the Muse 2 headset. Only about .00001% of the human brain has been mapped in full detail — against 27% of the ocean floor — and EEG is one of the few ways to collect brain data at scale, but a reading is often less than 30% clean. The project builds methods to collect and clean EEG on one of the most affordable headsets available, aiming at training-quality data for AI models and, longer term, at-home neurological screening that today needs hospital-grade hardware. Led by Zach Goldwyn with a team of six.

Canary

Adversarial prompt-injection detection across LLM tool-use pipelines. Prompt injection is rated the #1 risk for AI applications by OWASP, and work presented at NeurIPS finds that simple, low-effort human-written injections partially succeed against top-tier models in up to 86% of cases — while Gartner projects a 700% rise in enterprise applications carrying task-specific agents. Canary builds measurement tools for companies to benchmark their systems against injection, surface new attacks, and defend against them. Led by Marek Seablom-Michel with a team of six.

Social Simulation

Agentic orchestration and long-horizon tool-use evaluation, applied to beta-testing. 96% of companies surveyed across more than 260 organisations run some form of beta test, and its value comes from the diversity of the testers: viewing the same product through different cognitive frames surfaced inclusivity issues in over half of the interactions evaluated. Agents that simulate testers today struggle to hold distinct personalities, so the project grounds personas in real people rather than text prompts, adds cognitive perspectives such as impatience that haven't been modeled, and visualises the runs for non-technical clients. Led by Namish Mannepalli with a team of seven.

The team

The 2026–2027 team is 21 people: a director and two associate directors, three research leads, and sixteen researchers split across the three projects. It grew out of the 2025–2026 team, which was a single project of five that I led — two of whom, Zach Goldwyn and Saurish Suman, came back this year, Zach now leading a project of his own.

The culture

The program runs on three values. Impact: work on problems that haven't been solved, and give ambitious — even unrealistic — researchers the platform to try. Rigor: papers follow NeurIPS and IEEE frameworks and go through peer review by domain experts from industry and faculty. Aesthetics: when AI can generate almost anything quickly, work made with effort and care is what stands apart, and technology alone doesn't enrich anyone's life. The site itself is built to that last standard.
