I lead technical teams and build the systems myself. I was selected as incoming President of Cal Poly CSAI — the largest computing organization on campus, with 1,000+ members. Currently I serve as Director of Research, where I've spearheaded the development of the CS+AI Journal of Computer Science, which runs a full peer-review process, and the $1,000 CS+AI Research Scholarship Competition — a ten-week hackathon judged by faculty, industry, sponsors, and academics. I direct a team of associate directors and project leads across the club's research portfolio, defining project scope and success criteria to keep multi-team efforts on schedule.

I've spent two years doing AI/ML research in the Cal Poly Computer Science department. Most recently I led the autonomous navigation team, owning product vision and strategy for a four-phase system and iterating the unknown-object detection pipeline through four versions to 93% accuracy, while running four teams on an Agile cadence.

Before that I trained and optimized 10+ YOLO detection models on Google Cloud GPUs across a 1,000+ image dataset for a 30% mAP improvement, and integrated MiDaS monocular depth estimation as a $5k–$15k cheaper alternative to LiDAR — then took a Jetson Nano pipeline running two models from 2 to 15 FPS. My earlier research was on the data side: a web-mining pipeline over 400+ club and 2,000+ faculty records, vector-embedding indexing across 10,000+ records with cosine similarity search, and a 50% cut in LLM API costs by moving sequential calls to asynchronous batches.
